Penguins

Penguins (not the biscuit variety) are from the family of Spheniscidae. They belong to the order of Sphenisciformes.
Penguins cannot fly and have evolved 'flippers', sort of penguin arms that they swim with. They are very skilled swimmers.
These sea birds live in the Southern hemisphere

BUT... sadly, they can't survive in the UK



South Shetland Penguins

These islands are not the Shetland at the top of Scotland, but next to Antartica.

These islands are almost completely covered in ice and snow, a habitat loved by penguins.


Why are Penguin's so Special?

  1. Penguins are 60 Million years old
  2. The oldest living Penguin is 41 years old
  3. Emporor penguin's can dive over 500 metres down into the sea because of their bone density

Gus, decided to go on holiday to Australia. An emporor penguin turned up on a beach in Southern Australia on 1st November 2024. This beach was 2,200 miles away from his home in Antarctica. It is unknown why he swam there, but he was very underweight by the time he arrived. It is thought that he could not find food and so lost weight.




Emporor Penguin - How they have adapted?

The University of Southampton have studies Emporor penguins. They looked at how they have adapted over the last 30,000 years. The study talks about The Ross Sea area, where penguins have lived, even during the last ice age. It suggests that during the ice age, this was one of the few places in Antarctica where these penguins could survive. This area was a refuge for them. These penguins still live there in modern times.
